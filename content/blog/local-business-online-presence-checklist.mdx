# Online Presence for Local Businesses: The Complete Checklist

"Online presence" sounds like a buzzword. It's not. It's the answer to a simple question: **when a customer in your area searches for what you do, what do they find — and does it make them call you?**

For a local service business, your online presence is five pieces that work together. Miss any one, and you leak customers. Here's the checklist we run on every [free digital audit](/free-audit) we do.

## ☐ 1. A Website That Loads Fast and Works on a Phone

Your website is the hub — the one thing you own and control. Everything else points back to it.

The minimum viable version for a local business:

- Loads in under two seconds on a mobile connection
- Phone number visible without scrolling
- Five pages: Home, Services, About, Reviews, Contact
- Your service area and hours clearly stated
- A way to book or call after hours

If your site takes five seconds to load or hasn't been updated since 2018, you're paying hosting bills for a liability. Fast, mobile-first sites rank higher and convert more — and slow ones actively repel customers. Your [hosting provider sets the speed ceiling](/blog/why-hosting-matters), so this starts at the foundation.

**Don't have a website at all?** That's the single biggest gap in local business — and the fix is a one-day project, not a six-month one. (More below.)

## ☐ 2. A Complete Google Business Profile

This is the free listing that decides your map-pack fate. The full checklist:

- Business name, address, phone — exact and consistent
- Service area and hours set
- Categories chosen correctly
- Photos: logo, storefront, work samples, team
- Posts updated regularly (Google rewards active profiles)
- Questions answered

An incomplete profile is a dead listing. We've broken down the [full Google Business Profile setup, step by step](/blog/google-business-profile-checklist), so you can knock this out in an afternoon.

## ☐ 3. Reviews That Are Visible and Growing

Reviews are the social proof layer. Customers check them before they call — consistently, across nearly every age group.

- 10+ reviews on your Google profile (aim for 25+ within six months)
- Recent reviews — within the last 30 days, not 2019
- A system for asking every happy customer
- Responses to reviews — yes, the negative ones too

A steady review flow isn't luck; it's a [repeatable system](/blog/get-more-google-reviews). Businesses with 50+ reviews get dramatically more calls than businesses with four.

## ☐ 4. Consistent Info Everywhere Else

Your name, address, and phone number must match across every directory and platform — Google, Facebook, Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, yellow pages, trade association lists. Mismatched info confuses Google and destroys local rankings.

One wrong phone number on a stale directory can send your calls — and your customers — to whoever it belongs to. Wrong business details cost real money, and fixing them across the web is a [surprisingly expensive problem](/blog/business-listings-wrong-cost) if you let it fester.

## ☐ 5. A Way to Capture After-Hours Demand

Here's the one most businesses skip: **what happens when someone finds you at 9 PM?**

- Voicemail? The customer hangs up and calls the next result.
- Nobody? The customer assumes you're out of business.
- An [AI receptionist](/blog/missed-calls-cost) that answers, books, and confirms by text? The job is yours by morning.

Your online presence's whole job is to turn searches into scheduled work. If the front door is locked after hours, half the traffic you worked to attract walks to your competitor.

## The Order of Operations

If you're starting from zero, do it in this order:

1. **Website** — the hub everything points to
2. **Google Business Profile** — the map-pack entry ticket
3. **Reviews** — the trust layer
4. **Consistency cleanup** — stop the leaks
5. **After-hours capture** — turn presence into booked jobs

## What "Done" Looks Like

A customer searches "AC repair near me" at 9 PM, on their phone:

1. Your business appears in the top three, with a photo, 25+ reviews, and a website link
2. They tap through to a fast, clean site with your phone number front and center
3. They call — and **someone answers**, books the visit, and texts a confirmation

That's a complete online presence. It's not magic. It's five boxes checked.
