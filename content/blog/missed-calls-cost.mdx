# The $52,000 Missed-Call Problem: What Your After-Hours Voicemail Really Costs

Every service business has the same silent leak: calls that ring, go to voicemail, and never come back. It doesn't show up on any profit-and-loss statement, so nobody fixes it. But it's often the single biggest source of lost revenue in the entire business.

Let's put a number on it.

## The 30-Second Calculation

Grab a calculator. Three questions:

1. **How many calls do you miss in a typical week?** (Calls that go to voicemail, get ignored during busy hours, or come in after you close.) Be honest — most owners are surprised.
2. **What's the average value of one job?** (Your average ticket, including follow-up work and referrals.)
3. **Multiply: missed calls × job value × 52 weeks.**

Here's what that looks like for a typical HVAC company:

> 5 missed calls per week × $200 per job × 52 weeks = **$52,000 per year**

Not $5,000. **Fifty-two thousand dollars.** And that's the conservative version — it counts only the calls you *know* you missed. It doesn't count the calls that went to voicemail where the customer never left a message. Or the ones who called your competitor first and never called you at all.

## Why Customers Don't Leave Voicemails

The assumption behind a voicemail system is "customers will leave a message and we'll call back." The reality:

- **Only about 30% of callers leave a voicemail** when they reach one
- The rest hang up and call the next business on Google
- The next business answers → they get the job

An after-hours emergency isn't a "call us back tomorrow" situation. When the AC dies in a Texas August, the homeowner is calling down the list until someone answers. If you're voicemail, you're last.

## The Double Cost: This Week's Jobs + Next Year's Customers

Missed calls don't just lose the immediate job. Every un-answered call is a customer who tried you first, got ignored, and now trusts your competitor. They'll tell their neighbors. That's the cost that compounds — and it's invisible until you look.
