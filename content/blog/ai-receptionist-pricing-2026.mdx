# AI Receptionist Pricing in 2026: What It Costs and What You Actually Get

If you've looked into answering options recently, you've seen price tags ranging from $50/month to $2,000/month for the "same" service. They are not the same service. Here's an honest breakdown of what each option costs — and what it actually delivers.

## The Three Options

### Option 1: Hire a Human Receptionist

The most expensive option by far, and the one that still can't cover nights and weekends.

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual salary (full-time) | $30,000–$42,000 |
| Payroll taxes + benefits (+15%) | $4,500–$6,300 |
| **Effective monthly cost** | **$2,900–$4,000+** |
| Coverage | ~9 AM–5 PM, weekdays |

And when that receptionist is on lunch, on vacation, or out sick, the phones go to voicemail — the exact problem you hired them to solve.

### Option 2: Traditional Answering Service

Cheaper, but you get what you pay for.

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Monthly fee (24/7 coverage) | $200–$500 |
| Quality issues | Operators don't know your business |
| Message accuracy | Miss or garble 15–20% of details |
| Callback delay | 5–15 minutes per message relay |
| **Effective cost per useful lead** | Surprisingly high |

You're paying for a warm body that takes a message — not a receptionist that books the job.

### Option 3: AI Receptionist

| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Flat monthly price | **$497** |
| Setup fee | $0 |
| Coverage | **24/7, every day** |
| Answers in | ~2 seconds |
| Books appointments | Yes, on your calendar |
| Text confirmations | Yes, automatic |
| On-call paging | Yes, for emergencies |

## Why $497 Is the Number

Do the math honestly:

- **$497/month = 2–3 small jobs.** If the AI catches one after-hours job a week, it pays for itself.
- A traditional answering service at $380/month costs almost as much and *still* loses 3–4 leads a week to garbled messages.
- A human receptionist costs 6–8× more and only covers business hours.

The full system ($997/month) adds call analytics, automatic review requests after every job, and AI responses to your Google reviews — turning every completed job into a new 5-star review.

## What You Should NOT Pay For

- **Per-minute fees** — a receptionist doesn't bill by the minute. Neither should your answering.
- **Setup fees** — installation is a phone-forwarding setting. It should take minutes, not an installation invoice.
- **Long contracts** — if the service works, you'll stay because it works. Not because you're locked in.
