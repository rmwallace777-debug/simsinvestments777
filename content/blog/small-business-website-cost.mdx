# How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in 2026?

Ask five web designers what a small business website costs and you'll get five answers, from "$500" to "$15,000." They're all "right" — and none of them tells you what you actually need to know.

Here's the honest breakdown of what a small business website costs in 2026, what you're really paying for, and where the value actually is.

## The Three Options

### 1. DIY Builders: $15-40/month (plus your time)

Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy, and their cousins will happily sell you a template for $20-something a month. The real cost is hidden:

- **Your time** — 30-80 hours of fiddling, and it still looks like a template
- **The design gap** — generic layouts that don't build trust
- **The maintenance trap** — nobody updates it after month three, so it slowly rots
- **The speed problem** — DIY sites are notoriously slow, and speed is a ranking factor

The math works if you're handy and have the time. Most service business owners are busy running trucks, not websites — so the template sits half-finished.

### 2. Agency Builds: $3,000-10,000 (plus monthly retainers)

A proper agency will build you a gorgeous site — and bill you like it. Common reality:

- $5,000-$10,000 up front for a 6-12 week project
- $500+/month for "maintenance and SEO" (check what's actually included)
- Upcharges for every change you request
- Sales cycle built for the agency's pipeline, not your urgency

If you have a multi-location operation with a serious marketing budget, an agency can be worth every dollar. For a single-shop HVAC, plumbing, or electrical business, it's a lot of machine for a small job.

### 3. Done-For-You, Fixed-Price: $1,250 + $119/month

The middle path we built our website service around: a professional five-page site, **built and live in 24 hours**, at a fixed price with hosting and care included.

- **$1,250 one-time** — strategy, copy, design, build, launch
- **$119/month** — hosting, SSL, security, updates, backups, and support
- **No contracts** — cancel the care plan anytime; the site stays yours

You get the agency-grade outcome — fast, mobile-first, built for local search — without the agency timeline or retainers.

## What You're Actually Paying For

Price only makes sense against what it buys. Every option above needs to deliver four things:

### Speed
Google ranks fast sites higher, and over half of mobile visitors abandon sites that take more than three seconds to load. Whatever you pay, the site must load in under two seconds on a phone. If it doesn't, you paid for a liability.

### Mobile design
Over 60% of local searches happen on a phone. "Looks great on my desktop" is worthless — the site needs to work one-handed, on a jobsite, at 9 PM.

### Local search foundation
The site needs proper structure: clear service pages, service area, consistent NAP, schema, and fast hosting. That's what turns a brochure into a lead machine. It's also why [your hosting choice](/blog/why-hosting-matters) is part of the price — cheap hosting caps everything else.

### Someone who maintains it
The site that ranks in year one and still ranks in year four is the one that got updated — security patches, plugin updates, fresh content, speed checks. Maintenance isn't an upsell; it's the thing that keeps the site from becoming an embarrassment. "Set it and forget it" is how sites get hacked and deindexed.

- Your all-in website cost: $119/month after setup
- That's a **7x monthly return** — and the site only needs to work once to justify it

Missed after-hours calls are the same story: an [AI receptionist](/blog/missed-calls-cost) plus a findable website turns the calls you currently miss into booked jobs. The math compounds.

## What You Should NOT Pay For

- **"Guaranteed page-one rankings"** — nobody can guarantee that; anyone who does is lying
- **$500/month "SEO retainers" with no deliverables** — ask what you're getting every month
- **12-week timelines** — a five-page site doesn't take 12 weeks; it takes a day
- **Websites you don't own** — never rent your own storefront
