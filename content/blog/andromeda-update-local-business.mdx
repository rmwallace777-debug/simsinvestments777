# Andromeda Update: What Local Businesses Need to Know

If your Facebook ads "just stopped working" sometime in the last year — or you're thinking about starting ads for the first time — there's one thing you need to understand before spending another dollar: **Meta's Andromeda update changed the entire game.**

Here's what it is, what it means for local businesses, and exactly what to do about it. No jargon.

## The old way vs. the new way

**Before Andromeda (2014–2024):** you picked your audience. Homeowners, 35–65, interested in home improvement. Facebook found those people and showed them your ad. Your targeting choices decided who saw it.

**After Andromeda (2025–now):** Facebook's AI scans tens of millions of ads in milliseconds for every single user — and decides *which ad to show each person*. It reads your ad — the photo, the headline, the offer — and matches it against what that person actually responds to.

**Your ad creative IS your targeting now.** The photo and headline you choose decide who sees the ad, not the interest boxes you tick.

## What actually changed (plain-English version)

**1. Interest targeting is now a suggestion.** Those detailed interest layers you spent hours building? The algorithm treats them as a hint at best. Tight targeting actually limits what the AI can explore — broad targeting now routinely *outperforms* micro-segmented campaigns.

**2. Good ads burn out faster.** Ads that ran for weeks now die in days. The AI learns faster, so your winning ad gets exhausted sooner. You need a steady rotation of fresh creative — that's not optional anymore.

**3. Your Facebook page matters again.** Organic engagement and ad performance now feed one unified system. An inactive page genuinely drags down your ads. Posting regularly is no longer a vanity metric — it's infrastructure.

**4. Tracking is the secret lever.** Every conversion signal you feed Facebook (form fills, calls, bookings) trains the AI to find more people like your last customers. Businesses with proper tracking get cheaper leads; businesses without it pay more every month.

## The numbers that matter

- Facebook lead costs for local home services: **$5–25 per lead** — versus $100–230+ on Google for the same customer
- Cross-industry average ROAS on Meta: **5.3×** (lead-gen campaigns ~4.1×)
- A 2026 study of 3,014 advertisers ($834M in spend) found average ROAS **dropped 7%** during the rollout — and the worst-hit were the advertisers who'd been performing *best* before. The old playbook didn't survive contact with the new system
- Brands testing **20+ new ads per month** see **65% higher ROAS** than those testing fewer than 10

**What this really costs:** every month you run ads without conversion tracking, you're paying for Facebook to guess. With tracking, the algorithm compounds — it finds more people like your best customers, and your cost per lead trends down instead of up. That's the difference between $25 leads and $100+ leads.

## What to do about it (5 steps)

**1. Go broad.** Remove interest stacks. Keep location, age, and language only. Let the AI find your customers — the data says it's better at it than you were.

**2. Build 10–15 distinct ads.** Different hooks, not the same ad in different colors: seasonal offers, financing, "same-day service," before/after photos, customer stories, a founder video. Each angle is a different audience-finder.

**3. Use Instant Forms.** Lead ads with Instant Forms capture phone numbers without a landing page and let you ask qualification questions — so you're not paying for tire-kickers.

**4. Fix your tracking.** Install Google Tag Manager once in your site header, fire the Meta pixel through it, and set up custom conversion events (form submit, call, booking). This is the single highest-leverage fix. Server-side events take it further.

**5. Post consistently.** Three times a week minimum. Your page's organic signal feeds the same system your ads run on now.

## The honest caveats

- Interest targeting isn't *dead* — it's just downgraded from a rule to a suggestion. It still works sometimes, just less reliably than it used to.
- Nobody has "the" Andromeda answer — Meta keeps updating it (ads quality improved 14% after Q3 2025 tweaks alone). The strategy that works: broad targeting + creative volume + good tracking + regular refresh.
- The agencies promising "52% lower CPL" overnight are selling you a result, not a guarantee. The mechanics above are what actually moves the number.

## The bottom line

Andromeda didn't break Facebook ads — it moved the lever. **Creative quality and conversion tracking now decide your cost per lead, not audience targeting.** Local businesses that adapt are seeing the cheapest leads they've ever bought ($5–25). The ones still running 2024 playbooks are watching costs climb.
