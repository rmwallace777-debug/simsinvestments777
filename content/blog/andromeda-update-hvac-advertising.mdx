# What Meta's Andromeda Update Means for HVAC Advertising

If your Facebook ads suddenly stopped working in 2026, it's not a bug. It's Andromeda — Meta's new AI ad delivery system — and it changed the rules of the game for HVAC advertising.

Here's what changed, what it means for your AC business, and exactly how to adapt.

## The one sentence you need to remember

**Your ad creative is now your targeting.**

Before Andromeda (2014–2024), you told Facebook who to show your ads to: homeowners, 30–65, interested in home improvement. Facebook found those people and showed them your ad. Targeting was the lever.

Andromeda flipped it. Now Facebook scans **tens of millions of ads in milliseconds** and asks one question: *"which ad should this person see?"* It reads your ad — the photo, the headline, the hook, the offer — and matches it against what each user actually responds to.

You're no longer buying access to an audience. **You're buying algorithmic distribution based on your creative.** The ad itself picks your audience.

## What actually changed for HVAC advertisers

**1. Interest targeting is now a suggestion, not a rule.** Stacking interests ("home improvement" + "married with kids") used to be the play. Now the AI decides anyway — and tight targeting actually *limits* what the algorithm can explore. Broad targeting (Advantage+ Audience) now routinely **outperforms** the old micro-segmented campaigns.

**2. Ad fatigue hits in days, not weeks.** Andromeda learns faster, so your winning ad burns out faster — especially in B2B. One great ad isn't a campaign anymore. You need a **rotation**.

**3. The organic/paid wall is gone.** Your Facebook page's organic engagement and your ad performance now feed **one unified dataset**. An inactive page with no posts genuinely drags down your ads. Posting consistently isn't vanity anymore — it's ad infrastructure.

**4. Conversion events train the algorithm.** The more quality conversion signals you feed Facebook (form fills, calls, bookings), the better it finds people like your last customers. This is the single highest-leverage fix available.

## The numbers that matter for HVAC

- **Facebook HVAC CPL: $5–11 for service leads, $15–25 for install leads** — versus $104–231 per lead on Google Ads. Same customer, dramatically different entry cost.
- Median Facebook ROAS for HVAC: **1.65×**; top performers: **5.17×+**
- Meta's cross-industry average: **5.3× ROAS**; lead-gen campaigns ~**4.1×**
- Real case: 65,153 people reached for **$1,190 over 6 months, 27.8% conversion** (ResultCalls, 2026)
- Contractors who adapted to Andromeda: **52% lower cost per lead** (industry reports)

**What this really costs:** a $3,100–3,700 average HVAC job makes a $15–25 Facebook lead look cheap — but only if the tracking is right. Run ads without conversion events and you're paying Google prices for Facebook-quality targeting. That's the whole ballgame.

## How to fix your campaigns in 2026

**1. Go broad.** Remove interest stacks. Keep only location (your service area), age, and language. Let Andromeda find your customers — it's better at it than you were.

**2. Build a creative rotation — 10–15 distinct ads.** Different hooks: seasonal offers ("Same-day AC repair, $79 tune-up"), energy savings ("Cut your electric bill 40% with a variable-speed system"), emergency ("AC down in 100° heat?"), financing ("0% for 12 months on a new system"). Each angle is a different audience-finder.

**3. Use Instant Forms, not just links.** Lead ads with Instant Forms filter out tire-kickers and capture the phone number without a landing page. Set qualification questions so you're not paying for junk leads.

**4. Fix your tracking first, then scale.** Install Google Tag Manager once in your site header, fire your Meta pixel through it, and set up custom conversion events (form submit, call click, booking). Server-side events for the full funnel. Without this, Andromeda has nothing to learn from.

**5. Post on your page consistently.** Organic engagement feeds the same dataset now. Three posts a week minimum.

**6. Plan for fatigue.** Check your ad frequency weekly. When a winner's cost-per-action climbs, rotate in a fresh angle — don't just raise the budget.

## Seasonal offers outperform generic ads

The contractors winning on Facebook aren't competing with Google. They're using Meta to **plant the seed before the urgent moment** — maintenance offers, financing promos, energy-efficiency education — so that when the AC dies in August, the homeowner calls the name they already recognize.

## The bottom line

Andromeda didn't break Facebook ads. It revealed that creative — not targeting — was always the lever. HVAC companies with a strong creative rotation, broad targeting, and proper conversion tracking are seeing **lower CPLs than ever** ($5–25 vs $100+ on Google). The ones still running 2024 playbooks are paying more and getting less.
